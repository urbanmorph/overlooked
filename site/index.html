Seven gaps in what we know about species

All groups · India

Biologists name seven gaps in what we know about species (Hortal and others, 2015). Overlooked measures each gap for India where open data lets it, and adds the others as their data becomes open. How we measure them.

  1. Which species existLinnean gapIs every species described and named?–
  2. Where species liveWallacean gapDo we know where each species lives?–
  3. How many there arePrestonian gapDo we know how many of each species there are, and if that changes?–
  4. How species are relatedDarwinian gapDo we know each species’ place in the tree of life?–
  5. What species are likeRaunkiæran gapDo we know each species’ traits: size, life cycle, when it flowers or breeds?–
  6. What conditions species needHutchinsonian gapDo we know the climate and habitat each species can live in?–
  7. How species interactEltonian gapDo we know what each species eats, pollinates or lives on?–

Wait for a name:

Observations on iNaturalist that the observer named to species and that nobody has confirmed yet. Species at the top matter most: not on the India list yet, no open record here yet, threatened, or endemic. Open them on iNaturalist to agree or to give a better name (you need an iNaturalist account).

Observations on iNaturalist named only to genus. A person who knows the genus can often name the species. Genera at the top matter most: not on the India list yet (a possible new record for India), or with species that have no open record in India.

Observations on iNaturalist named only to a family, an order or a higher level. A person who knows the group can often name the genus or the species. Each link opens the observations of one group.
