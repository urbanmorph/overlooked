Sources and method

Each week a scheduled job takes fresh data from the sources, computes every figure again, and publishes the result as a dated snapshot. Nobody edits the figures by hand. A snapshot never changes after it is published.

The weekly run

  1. 01Check the sourcesOne request to each source. If GBIF or the data store does not answer, the run stops.
  2. 02DownloadGBIF downloads, one after another, each with its own DOI. iNaturalist species lists per state. The checklists and the IUCN Red List when they change.
  3. 03PlaceEach record goes to its LGD district by its coordinates.
  4. 04ComputeThe master list: every species from every source, with which species have an open record, which observations wait for a name, and the IUCN status, for India and each state.
  5. 05CheckEvery download has a DOI. Large changes from the last run stop the run.
  6. 06PublishThe files go to the data store. The site reads them in your browser.

The job can run again safely. It does not repeat finished steps, and it does not send a file again if the data store already has the same file. If GBIF reports no change since the last run, the week downloads nothing.

What the words mean

Documented
A checklist, a paper or an open record gives the species for India: a ZSI or BSI checklist, Kew's plant list, Plazi, BSI Plant Discoveries, ZSI Animal Discoveries, or an open record. A listing by BSI or ZSI counts. Most species with no open record are known from papers and from specimens that are not online yet.
Open record
A record of the species from India that anyone can check online: a GBIF record (a specimen, a photo, a sighting, a sound, a DNA sample, or a record from a paper), or an iNaturalist research-grade observation. This is the rule of GBIF, which takes only research-grade observations from iNaturalist. An iNaturalist observation that waits for a name is not an open record of its species yet.
Described
The year the species was first described. For a species described from India since 2007: the year of the paper (Plazi, BSI Plant Discoveries, ZSI Animal Discoveries). Else the year in the Catalogue of Life author citation, else in the ZSI checklist, else in Kew's plant list (for a plant renamed later, the year of its first name). Most fungi, lichens and mosses have no year in these sources.
No record since it was described
No GBIF record of the species from India after the year of its description. This is checked only for species described since 2007 that the yearly BSI Plant Discoveries and ZSI Animal Discoveries editions or Plazi list. For a species that an edition lists in a later year than its description (for example a new record for India), it is not checked.
IUCN status
The category on the IUCN Red List (CR, EN, VU, NT, LC, DD), or not assessed. Threatened means CR, EN or VU.
Waiting for a name
An iNaturalist observation with no agreed name yet. The observer may have named it to species, to genus only, or only to a family or a higher level. A species that only such observations name is not on the India list yet; when someone confirms the name, it goes on the list.
Name not matched
A source name that does not match a Catalogue of Life name. It stays on the list under its own name, with this label.
District and state
LGD boundaries. A GBIF record goes to a district by its coordinates, rounded to 3 decimals (about 110 m). A point less than 5 km from land goes to the nearest district. A record that cannot go in a district goes to a state when its state name is clear, else it counts only for India. These records have no coordinates, coordinates that are uncertain by more than 25 km, or coordinates that GBIF marks as possibly wrong. GBIF records with coordinates outside India are not used. Records whose state name is a place outside India (for example Nepal, Ceylon or Indiana), and datasets that are not from India although GBIF has them as India, are not used; the lists are in the source code (pipeline/ref). DNA sequences with no coordinates are not used, because their dates are often the date the sequence was sent.
No exact location
The species has an open record from India, but no record of it can go in a district (see District and state).
Only GBIF records with no exact location
No exact location, and GBIF is the only source that lists the species for India. Most are old museum specimens. One source and no map point is weak evidence, so check the records before you use the species.
Records with no coordinates
Some GBIF records of the species from India have no coordinates. The publisher can add them.
Records with coordinates uncertain by more than 25 km
Some GBIF records give coordinates, but the publisher says they are uncertain by more than 25 km: too coarse for a district.
Records with coordinates that GBIF marks as possibly wrong
GBIF found a problem with the coordinates of some records, for example swapped or zero values. Such a record may not be from India. Check it with the publisher.
Recorded under the name as published
GBIF has records of the species from India after its description, but only under the name as the paper gives it: GBIF has not added the name to its species list yet. These records are an open record of the species in India, in no state.

Records used

  • Country: India, with coordinates and no known problem with the place
  • Status: present
  • Uncertainty of the place: 25 km or less
  • No fossils, and no living specimens in zoos or gardens
  • Some datasets are not used, for example gene-bank and compiled crop data
  • DNA sequence records count. A species known only from DNA sequences has the label "DNA only". Host-organism records from sequence archives are not used

Names follow the Catalogue of Life, as GBIF uses it. Species links go to the Catalogue of Life.

Shortlists: the test Experimental

The Shortlists page gives a short list of names for observations that wait on iNaturalist. A model makes each list: BioCLIP 2.5, with a classifier trained on research-grade photos from India. A person checks every name on iNaturalist. We post nothing. A list never changes a status, a count or a label on this site.

We tested the lists on 2,545 research-grade observations from India, from observers whose photos the model did not learn from. Each waited 30 days or more for research grade. Overall, the first name was right for 84.4%. A list made to hold the right name 90% of the time held it 92.0% of the time, with 1 name in the middle case. On observations that still wait the lists do less well, because these are harder.

Which groups are on the page

We wrote these rules on 20 October 2026, before we drew the test data, and we did not change them after.

  • A group needs 100 test observations or more. Algae, ferns, fish, lichens, and mosses and liverworts have fewer, so they are not on the page.
  • Quick to confirm (up to 3 species): the list holds the right species 88% of the time or more, the middle list has 2 names or fewer, and the first name is right 80% of the time or more.
  • Genus first (up to 5 genera): else, the genus list holds the right genus 88% of the time or more, with 2 genera or fewer in the middle case.
  • Else the group is for an expert, and it is not on the page.
  • The model may have learned from older photos. So for each group we check the observations that became research grade in 2025 or later. If there are 40 or more, and the list holds the right name less than 85% of the time, the group goes down one band. Grasses and sedges went down (81.3% on 50 observations). Bees, wasps and ants (85.3%) and moths (87.6%) are close to the line.

The test that comes next

On 20 October 2026 we saved the lists for 8,044 observations that waited in the India queue (made in the last 120 days, up to 400 per group). These observations are never on the Shortlists page. We check them again on or after 19 November 2026, 19 December 2026 and 18 January 2027: how many got a confirmed name, and how often that name was in the list. Then we compare them with the observations that the page shows. The difference is the effect of the page. The saved file has the SHA-256 checksum c395de2a16730e31338b02953f371fe8435cd20d606cb99724d0661d2e523398, recorded before the first check.

To see if the page helps, we count the links that people follow from it to iNaturalist, per group, as one number per day. We keep no data about a visitor.
